# Geometry

The shape of a cell: what is measured, how, and how the measured shape relates
to the nominal one.

An index names a cell; this section is about what that cell actually is on the
ellipsoid. Three themes run through it. The cells are parallelograms whose lean
is a measured quantity rather than a constant. The metrics defined over them
are transcriptions of published conventions, and each one states where the
convention stops being trustworthy. And the area a cell is specified to have
and the area its boundary encloses are separate numbers, which agree almost
everywhere and must not be assumed to.
